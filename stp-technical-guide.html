STP Plant Engineering Guide

Engineering reference for sewage treatment plant design, biological process control, clarification, disinfection and sludge handling.

Open detailed PDF guide Ask an engineer

Sewage characterization

Peak flow, BOD, COD, TSS, ammonia and variability determine hydraulic and biological design.

Biological treatment

Maintain the required oxygen transfer, biomass concentration and sludge age for the selected process.

Clarification and disinfection

Secondary clarification must protect downstream disinfection; UV or chemical disinfection is selected to meet reuse or discharge requirements.

Operations

Routine checks of DO, pH, MLSS, settling and sludge wasting provide early warning of process instability.

STP sizing and operating considerations

Estimate sewage flow from the served population or facility occupancy, water use, peak factors and operating schedule. Characterize influent BOD, COD, TSS, pH, ammonia and any industrial contribution before selecting the process. Hotel, residential, institutional and mixed-use sewage can vary significantly by hour and day, so equalization and peak-flow management should be evaluated.

Process and performance checks

  • Confirm screening, equalization and pumping arrangements are appropriate for peak hydraulic load.
  • For biological processes, trend dissolved oxygen, pH, mixed-liquor suspended solids where relevant, sludge age and settling behaviour.
  • Check secondary clarification for sludge carryover and investigate changes in blanket depth or effluent turbidity.
  • Choose disinfection based on required reuse, storage and discharge conditions; verify contact or UV system performance as applicable.
  • Provide a defined route for excess sludge thickening, dewatering and disposal.

Common symptoms and first checks

Persistent odour may indicate septic conditions, poor aeration, excessive sludge accumulation or hydraulic dead zones. High suspended solids can result from hydraulic overload, poor settling or process instability. Foaming can have several causes, so identify the source before adding defoamer. Compare field observations with laboratory results and operating records before changing the process.
