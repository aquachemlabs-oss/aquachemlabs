Cooling Tower Cycles of Concentration: Practical Guide

How cycles of concentration affect cooling-tower water use, chemistry, blowdown and scaling risk.

Balancing cycles and water use

Cycles of concentration compare dissolved constituents in recirculating cooling water with those in make-up water. Increasing cycles can reduce make-up and blowdown but concentrates scale-forming and corrosive species.

Conductivity can be used as a control signal when it correlates reliably with the water chemistry being managed. Chemical treatment, metallurgy and microbiological conditions must still be checked.

The practical target is the highest stable cycle that meets scale, corrosion and microbiological control requirements without causing excessive fouling.

How to estimate cycles of concentration

A practical estimate compares a relatively conservative dissolved constituent in recirculating water with the same constituent in make-up water. Conductivity is commonly used as a control proxy, but the relationship should be confirmed because treatment chemicals, process contamination and changing make-up chemistry can affect the reading.

Illustrative calculation: if recirculating-water conductivity is 2,400 µS/cm and make-up conductivity is 400 µS/cm, the simple conductivity ratio is 6. This is only an initial estimate; it is not a guarantee that six cycles is chemically safe. Confirm the value with appropriate chemistry, system conditions and the treatment programme.

Blowdown and make-up balance

As cycles increase, blowdown and make-up demand can decrease, but dissolved constituents also concentrate. Scale risk depends on hardness, alkalinity, silica, pH, temperature and surface heat flux. Corrosion and microbiological control must be assessed alongside scale potential. Automated conductivity blowdown control is useful only when the sensor is clean, calibrated and located where the sample represents the circulating system.

Operating checklist

  • Trend make-up and circulating conductivity at a consistent temperature basis.
  • Check pH, hardness/alkalinity, silica and inhibitor residuals against the site programme.
  • Inspect heat-transfer surfaces, side-stream filtration and corrosion-monitoring results.
  • Review blowdown meter accuracy and investigate sudden changes in make-up demand.
