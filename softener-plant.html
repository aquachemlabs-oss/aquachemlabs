Water Softener Plant Design & Service

Ion-exchange softening removes calcium and magnesium hardness that can cause scale in boilers, heat exchangers, membranes and process equipment.

Engineering approach

Control hardness before scale forms

A softener does not remove all dissolved salts; it primarily exchanges hardness ions for sodium. Correct sizing depends on hardness load, flow, regeneration interval and allowable outlet hardness for the process.

Design & operating considerations

Hardness load

Sizing should use actual hardness concentration and daily water demand.

Regeneration

Salt dose, brine concentration and regeneration sequence affect exchange capacity.

Pretreatment

Iron, manganese, turbidity and organics can foul resin and reduce performance.

Leakage control

Routine outlet hardness testing catches exhaustion and valve-sequence problems early.

Frequently asked questions

What does a water softener remove?

It primarily exchanges calcium and magnesium hardness for sodium ions. It does not replace RO or demineralisation when dissolved-salt reduction is required.

Why does hardness leak from a softener?

Possible causes include resin exhaustion, inadequate regeneration, channeling, valve sequencing faults, resin fouling or excessive service flow.

Should a softener be tested every day?

For critical applications, frequent outlet-hardness checks are a practical way to detect breakthrough before it reaches downstream equipment.
