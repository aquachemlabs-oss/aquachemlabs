Pretreatment

Feed quality and pretreatment protect ion-exchange and EDI modules from hardness, organics and particulates.

DI systems

Mixed-bed or separate-bed ion exchange produces high-purity water when regeneration and rinsing are controlled.

EDI systems

Electrodeionization continuously removes ions using electricity, resin and selective membranes without chemical regeneration in the EDI module.

DI and EDI: choosing a polishing approach

Deionization reduces dissolved ionic species using ion exchange. Conventional cation/anion trains, mixed beds and electrodeionization are not interchangeable choices; selection depends on feed-water quality, required conductivity and silica limits, continuous-flow requirements, regeneration logistics and the availability of suitable pretreatment. EDI normally requires controlled feed quality and should be evaluated against the manufacturer's feed-water limits.

Engineering inputs

  • Feed conductivity, hardness, alkalinity, silica, TOC and oxidant levels, as relevant to the selected process.
  • Required product-water conductivity/resistivity, silica limit, flow and allowable downtime.
  • Upstream RO performance and the stability of permeate quality.
  • Regeneration chemical storage, neutralization, rinse-water demand and waste handling for regenerable systems.

Quality assurance

Use calibrated conductivity or resistivity instruments and sample at a defined, representative point. Temperature compensation and sample contamination can affect readings, especially at very low conductivity. Trend quality over a full service cycle rather than relying on a single reading. If quality deteriorates, check upstream RO performance, flow distribution, regeneration or EDI operating conditions, instrument calibration and sample handling before changing resin or equipment.
