WTP design basis and routine performance checks

A water treatment plant should be designed around the source-water analysis and the quality required at the point of use. Surface water, borewell water and mixed sources can differ significantly, and seasonal turbidity or contamination events may change the required treatment train. Confirm average and peak flow, storage, operating hours, raw-water variability and whether treated water feeds potable use, utilities, process equipment or RO pretreatment.

Typical design inputs

  • Turbidity, suspended solids, colour, pH, hardness, alkalinity, iron, manganese and conductivity/TDS.
  • Microbiological requirements and disinfection needs where relevant to the intended use.
  • Filter loading, backwash availability, chemical storage and sludge handling.
  • Required outlet quality, monitoring points, automation and operator access.

Operations

Trend raw and treated turbidity, filter differential pressure, backwash frequency, pH and any application-specific parameters. Investigate rapid filter clogging, poor clarification or breakthrough by checking raw-water changes, chemical preparation and dosing, mixing, settling, filter media condition and backwash performance. Chemical dose and process settings must be confirmed through testing and the actual source-water conditions.
