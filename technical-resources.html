Technical Resources for Water & Wastewater Treatment

A practical reference layer for plant managers, engineering teams and procurement professionals. Use it to prepare a technical enquiry, compare treatment approaches and identify the operating data required before final equipment or chemical selection.

Start with the data

A good treatment design begins with a good water-quality picture.

For an initial review, provide the source water or effluent analysis, required flow, operating hours, target treated-water quality and any existing plant information. Final process selection, chemical programme and equipment sizing should be confirmed against actual site conditions.

1. Water analysis

Typical decision inputs include pH, TDS/conductivity, hardness, alkalinity, silica, iron, turbidity and—where relevant—COD, BOD, TSS, oil & grease, colour and microbiological parameters.

2. Capacity

State average and peak flow, daily operating hours, storage available and whether the system is continuous, batch or campaign based.

3. Product-water target

Define the actual end use: process water, cooling make-up, boiler feed, utility water, reuse or another specified quality. The target determines the treatment train.

4. Existing plant

Share P&IDs, equipment nameplates, membrane data, chemical consumption, operating trends, photos and recent test results when available.

Water efficiency

Design for reuse, not just treatment.

Industrial water systems can often be improved by separating water qualities, reducing unnecessary losses, controlling cooling-tower concentration, and evaluating treated-water reuse. The appropriate route depends on water chemistry, process requirements, economics and applicable permissions.

Cooling towers

Monitor concentration and blowdown

Water-efficiency guidance highlights cycles-of-concentration management, side-stream filtration and automated chemical-feed/blowdown controls as potential opportunities where appropriate.

Reuse

Match quality to end use

Reuse planning should start from the actual end-use water-quality requirement rather than treating every stream to the same specification.

Compliance

Use the applicable standard

Drinking-water applications in India should be evaluated against the applicable BIS specification; IS 10500:2012 is the published drinking-water standard referenced by BIS.
