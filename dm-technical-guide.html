Ion exchange

Cation and anion beds remove dissolved ions to produce low-conductivity water for industrial processes.

Regeneration

Acid and alkali regeneration restores exchange capacity; chemical handling and rinse quality are critical.

Monitoring

Track conductivity, silica, pressure drop and regeneration performance to identify exhaustion or leakage.

DM plant design and regeneration control

Demineralisation systems use ion-exchange resins to reduce dissolved ionic contaminants. The treatment train depends on feed conductivity, alkalinity, hardness, silica, required product-water quality, flow profile and whether a mixed-bed polisher or downstream process is needed. Confirm the required outlet quality and sampling point before sizing vessels and resin volumes.

What to monitor

  • Feed and product conductivity, and silica where it is a critical quality parameter.
  • Service flow, pressure drop, treated volume between regenerations and any leakage trend.
  • Acid and caustic concentration, regeneration flow, contact time and rinse quality, using the resin and equipment supplier's procedure.
  • Feed pretreatment performance, because suspended solids, oxidants, organics and iron can reduce resin performance.

Regeneration and troubleshooting

Exhaustion may appear as rising conductivity or silica leakage before the expected run length. Check sample integrity, instruments, valve sequencing, chemical strength, flow distribution and rinse completion before assuming the resin must be replaced. Incomplete regeneration, channeling, fouling or incorrect service flow can cause premature breakthrough. Regeneration chemicals are hazardous and must be handled with suitable PPE, containment, ventilation and site procedures.
