Effect of Temperature on Water Conductivity | Engineering Note

How temperature changes measured water conductivity and why temperature compensation matters in industrial water treatment.

Temperature and conductivity

Electrical conductivity generally rises as temperature increases because ionic mobility changes. A conductivity value should therefore be interpreted with its measurement temperature and, where appropriate, a temperature-compensated value.

For RO, DM and boiler applications, trends are more useful when measurements are taken consistently. A sudden conductivity change at a stable temperature can indicate leakage, exhaustion, contamination or instrumentation problems.

Use the instrument's stated compensation method and calibration procedure; do not compare readings from different instruments without checking temperature and cell configuration.

How temperature compensation affects field readings

Electrical conductivity reflects the concentration and mobility of ions in water. As temperature rises, ionic mobility generally increases, so the measured conductivity of the same sample typically rises. A meter with automatic temperature compensation estimates conductivity at a reference temperature using its configured compensation model; it does not remove the need to record actual sample temperature.

Good measurement practice

  • Record both raw conductivity and sample temperature when troubleshooting a change in water quality.
  • Use the same reference temperature and compensation settings when comparing historical trends.
  • Calibrate the probe using a suitable certified standard and follow the meter manufacturer's instructions.
  • Check probe fouling, air bubbles, cable connections, cell constant and sample contamination if readings drift.
  • For very low conductivity water, use a suitable low-conductivity cell, clean sampling containers and appropriate sample handling.

Why this matters in treatment plants

In RO systems, permeate conductivity trends can help detect changes in salt rejection, but flow, pressure, temperature and instrument condition should be considered together. In DM and mixed-bed systems, conductivity breakthrough can signal exhaustion or leakage, while boiler and cooling-water controls use conductivity for different purposes and setpoints. Never compare values from different instruments without checking units, calibration and compensation.
