Engineering approach

Manage cycles, biology and corrosion together

As water evaporates, dissolved minerals concentrate. Treatment must therefore balance cycles of concentration, blowdown, scale inhibition, corrosion control and biocide effectiveness.

  • Cycles of concentration
  • Scale inhibition
  • Corrosion control
  • Biocide programme
  • Blowdown control
  • Conductivity monitoring
  • ORP / microbiology
  • Heat-transfer protection

Design & operating considerations

Cycles of concentration

Higher cycles can save water but increase scaling and corrosion risk if chemistry is not controlled.

Biocide control

Oxidising or non-oxidising programmes should be selected around system conditions and microbiological risk.

Monitoring

Conductivity, pH, inhibitor residuals and microbiological indicators provide feedback for the treatment programme.

Mechanical condition

No chemical programme can compensate for blocked strainers, poor flow distribution or heavily fouled heat-exchange surfaces.

Frequently asked questions

What are cycles of concentration?

They describe how concentrated the recirculating water becomes relative to the makeup water, often tracked using conductivity or another conservative tracer.

Why does cooling-tower conductivity rise?

Evaporation removes water but leaves dissolved salts behind. Without sufficient blowdown, those salts accumulate.

Can chemical dosing alone fix cooling problems?

Not always. Mechanical fouling, poor circulation, inadequate blowdown, drift issues or tower fill condition may also need attention.
