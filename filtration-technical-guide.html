Filter selection and operating checks

Industrial filtration may use pressure or gravity media filters, activated carbon, cartridge filters, bag filters or application-specific polishing. Selection depends on the particle-size distribution, turbidity, solids loading, required outlet quality, flow rate, allowable pressure drop and downstream equipment sensitivity. A filter is not a substitute for upstream clarification when solids loading is excessive.

What to define in the design basis

  • Raw-water quality and its variation during monsoon, source changes or process upsets.
  • Required flow, peak flow, filtration velocity and available filter area.
  • Media type and depth, underdrain arrangement, backwash rate and backwash-water availability.
  • Target outlet turbidity or particle quality and the acceptable differential-pressure limit.
  • Downstream protection requirements, including cartridge filtration before RO where applicable.

Maintenance and troubleshooting

Record inlet/outlet pressure or differential pressure, outlet turbidity, service run time and backwash frequency. Early breakthrough may indicate channeling, incorrect backwash, exhausted or fouled media, damaged internals or an abrupt increase in solids loading. Confirm that backwash flow expands the media appropriately for the installed system and water temperature; use the equipment supplier's specified rates. Activated carbon systems also require a clear strategy for microbial control and replacement or reactivation.
