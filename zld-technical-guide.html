ZLD process design and recovery trade-offs

Zero liquid discharge projects combine wastewater treatment, concentration and final solids management so that a liquid effluent stream is not discharged from the defined process boundary. The train is site-specific: wastewater composition, flow variability, dissolved salts, silica, hardness, organics, temperature sensitivity, energy availability and the final solids route all influence feasibility and operating cost.

Typical process blocks

  • Equalization and contaminant-specific pretreatment to protect downstream membranes and thermal equipment.
  • Biological or physicochemical treatment where needed to reduce organics, suspended solids or targeted contaminants.
  • Membrane concentration, evaporative concentration or a combined approach where technically and economically appropriate.
  • Crystallization, dewatering and managed handling of the concentrated salt/solid stream.
  • Monitoring and reuse of recovered water only after its quality is confirmed suitable for the intended use.

Critical feasibility questions

Confirm whether salts are compatible with the proposed concentration equipment, how scale-forming constituents will be managed, what the expected condensate/permeate quality is, and where the final solids can legally and safely be handled. Recovery percentages should be calculated from a defined water balance and should not be promised without feed characterization and pilot or vendor design data. Evaluate energy, chemical consumption, cleaning, corrosion, availability and lifecycle cost—not only water recovery.
